GAUSSIAN VS POISSON SELECTION REGIME

In radiation metrology, the Poisson distribution governs discrete nuclear decay events. When the average count rate is high ($n > 20$), the Poisson distribution asymptotically converges to the continuous Gaussian (Normal) distribution. For low counts ($n \le 20$, such as natural background or 0-19 range events), the discrete asymmetric Poisson distribution must be utilized.

VARIANCE EQUALS MEAN CHARACTERISTIC (s² ≈ n)

A defining mathematical hallmark of the Poisson distribution is that its variance is equal to its mean ($\sigma^2 = n$). The ratio of sample variance to mean ($s^2/n$, Fano factor) approaching 1.0 serves as strong physical verification of purely random radioactive disintegration.

DEGREES OF FREEDOM & ESTIMATED PARAMETERS

For the Chi-Square goodness-of-fit test:
• Gaussian Model: $\nu = K - 1 - 2 = K - 3$ (estimating both mean $n$ and standard deviation $\sigma$).
• Poisson Model: $\nu = K - 1 - 1 = K - 2$ (estimating only mean parameter $n$).

RECURRENCE RELATION IN POISSON DISTRIBUTION

Instead of computing large factorials directly, the Poisson probabilities can be iteratively calculated using the recurrence formula:
$P(0) = e^{-n}$
$P(m+1) = \frac{n}{m+1} P(m)$
This recurrence relation drastically simplifies numerical evaluation and prevents factorial arithmetic overflow.
